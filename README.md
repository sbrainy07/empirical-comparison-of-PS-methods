# Empirical Comparison of Tree-Based Machine Learning Models for Estimating Generalized Propensity Scores in Multi-Category Treatment Settings

**Oluwaseun Farotimi, Ph.D.** • **Deborah K. Reed, Ph.D.**
University of Tennessee, Knoxville | College of Education, Health, and Human Sciences

## Abstract

Propensity score (PS) methods address confounding in observational research by balancing measured covariates across treatment groups. Using an applied teacher professional development (PD) example with three PD types, we compare four tree-based machine learning methods — classification tree (CART), bagging, random forest (RF), and gradient boosted models (GBM) — for estimating generalized propensity scores.

## Research Question

How well do different tree-based ML models achieve covariate balance across multiple treatments?

## Data

19,065 kindergarteners and 1,254 teachers in two large urban districts who completed the Fall 2023 and Spring 2024 universal reading screener (URS) administrations.

## Key Findings

- GBM and RF achieved the best overall covariate balance after weighting.
- CART and Bagging produced maximum ASMD above 0.20 and poor balance after weighting.
- GBM's sequential optimization of treatment-assignment prediction outperforms the simpler, interpretable CART and Bagging models.
